The thinking in brief.

AIconomy Institute has the opportunity to establish a lasting institutional presence around the economic consequences of artificial intelligence. The World AIconomy Forum can act as its flagship convening platform, while the Institute provides continuity and a year-round voice.

Build an authoritative, contemporary and globally relevant brand rooted in an Indian perspective.

◆ [ 01 ] The opportunity

More than a forum. An institution.

AIconomy Institute has the opportunity to build a recognisable institutional voice around how artificial intelligence is changing economies, industries, institutions and the way value is created.

The World AIconomy Forum can serve as its flagship convening platform, bringing together leaders and perspectives, while the Institute provides continuity, a point of view and a platform for ideas beyond a single event.

[ A possible positioning ]

An India-founded, globally oriented institution exploring how artificial intelligence is reshaping the economy, and bringing together the people, ideas and perspectives needed to navigate that transformation.

A possible brand architecture
Parent institution
AIconomy Institute

Continuity, point of view and a platform for ideas beyond a single event.

Flagship platform
World AIconomy Forum

The convening platform, bringing together leaders and perspectives.

As confirmed
Future initiatives

Research, programmes and initiatives brought under the same brand system as they are confirmed.

Initial brand attributes
01 Authoritative, but not intimidating.
02 Intellectual, but not overly academic.
03 Global in outlook, with a clear perspective from India.
04 Forward-looking, without relying on AI hype.
05 Institutional and enduring, rather than event-led or trend-driven.

◆ [ 02 ] Landscape review

A landscape of conveners and agenda-setters.

Our initial scan suggests AIconomy Institute would enter an active ecosystem. Existing platforms already bring together government, industry, researchers and global stakeholders around AI, technology policy and the future of innovation. The question is therefore not simply who else hosts an AI event, but what distinct institutional role AIconomy can credibly own.

ReferenceRoleWhat it signalsRelevance
India AI Impact Summit 2026 Ecosystem / national convening A government-led platform framing AI through people, planet and progress: economic growth, access, inclusion, safety, talent and science. Important ecosystem context; not a like-for-like competitor.
Carnegie India, Global Technology Summit Convening / agenda-setting A flagship forum co-hosted with India’s Ministry of External Affairs, examining technology and geopolitics. A benchmark for thematic clarity, credibility and convening.
Observer Research Foundation Research / convening Combines policy research, public commentary and convening, extending dialogue into analysis and public output. A reference for connecting dialogue with ongoing research.
Inc42 AI Summit / ET Edge AI Summit Industry-event landscape Examples of the wider commercial event space, showing that AI-focused conferences are already common. Reinforces the need for an enduring purpose beyond a one-off event.
What we take from the landscape
01Convening alone may not be sufficiently distinctive; the market already has established AI and technology forums.
02Credibility comes from a clear institutional point of view, credible voices and consistency, not just event scale.
03Research, public communication and convening reinforce one another when connected by a clear theme.
04An India-rooted perspective can be globally relevant when it addresses questions of wider economic and policy significance.

◆ [ 03 ] Strategic opportunity

Where AIconomy creates distinction.

A potential point of difference is to focus on the economic transformation driven by AI, not AI as a technology category alone. This creates a more specific lens through which the Institute can frame its conversations and public identity.

01

Economic transformation

Productivity, work, value creation and the changing structure of industries.

02

Capital & competitiveness

Investment, infrastructure, markets and the competitive position of economies.

03

Policy & governance

Regulation, institutions, public policy and responsible adoption.

04

Global collaboration

Connecting Indian and international perspectives across sectors and geographies.

A potential institutional model
Convene

Bring together

Relevant leaders, practitioners, researchers and policymakers.

Interpret

Develop a view

A clear perspective on the economic and societal implications of AI.

Amplify

Carry ideas forward

Into research, insight and public communication beyond the flagship forum.

[ A hypothesis for discussion ] This model connects three mutually reinforcing roles. The Institute’s actual programmes, research capabilities and publishing commitments should be confirmed before they are presented as established activities.

These ten workstreams form the proposed scope of the brand identity engagement. Each will be developed through discussion, exploration and approval; the descriptions outline intended areas of work rather than predetermined outcomes.

Brand ideology & vision

Define the underlying belief, purpose and long-term ambition: what the Institute exists to contribute and the change it hopes to influence.

02

Brand strategy & positioning

Establish the Institute’s role, differentiation, value proposition and relationship with the World AIconomy Forum.

03

Competitor research & analysis

Review relevant institutions, conveners and platforms to identify positioning opportunities and avoid a generic category identity.

04

Target audience & brand personality

Clarify priority audiences and the qualities, tone and character the brand should consistently express.

05

Brand story & messaging

The core narrative: how the Institute introduces itself, explains its relevance and communicates its work.

06

Logo design

A distinctive primary identity, with supporting configurations for institutional and Forum use.

07

Colour palette & typography

A considered colour and type direction supporting recognition, legibility and a consistent institutional presence.

08

Visual identity & design language

The wider visual system: graphic elements, layout principles, imagery direction and flexibility across formats.

09

Brand applications & reusable pitch decks

How the identity translates into key touchpoints, including presentation layouts and reusable deck components.

10

Brand guidelines & identity manual

A practical guide bringing the approved identity together, covering its core elements and consistent application.

◆ [ 05 ] Initial creative territory

Institutional intelligence.

Our initial creative territory is a visual identity that combines the confidence of a global institution with the clarity and energy of a modern platform for ideas.

What this could look and feel like
01 A confident, typographic identity with a distinctive and flexible logo system.
02 A restrained, contemporary colour direction with a memorable accent.
03 Structured layouts informed by editorial design, research and data communication.
04 Graphic elements that suggest systems, connections, exchange and economic networks.
05 A visual language that works across the Institute, the Forum and future initiatives.
[ What we would avoid ] Predictable AI clichés: robot imagery, glowing brains, generic circuit-board graphics and overused futuristic effects. The aim is to build a more ownable and enduring institutional language.
The intended balance
Institutional

Credible and composed

Enduring, considered and appropriate to an institution with a long-term role.

Contemporary

Clear and flexible

Relevant across digital and physical touchpoints as the platform grows.

Intelligent

Editorial and grounded

Evidence-aware and rooted in ideas rather than visual effects.

◆ [ 05A ] The design system

A practical system, not a collection of assets.

The identity should be supported by a reusable design system: a set of visual rules, components and templates that helps the Institute and its Forum communicate consistently as their activities grow. It translates the approved brand direction into repeatable decisions across digital, presentation and communication formats.

01 / Foundations

Core elements

Colour tokens and usage rules; typography hierarchy; spacing and sizing scale; grid and layout principles; shape, line and graphic treatments; iconography; image direction; contrast and legibility guidance.

02 / Identity behaviour

Logo system

Primary and secondary configurations; clear space and minimum sizes; light and dark applications; placement principles; guidance for how the Institute and Forum identities work together.

03 / Graphic language

Data visualisation

A consistent approach to diagrams, charts, data callouts, maps, timelines, quote treatments and other information-led graphics, particularly relevant to economic and technology themes.

04 / Composition

Layout and grids

Page and screen grids, alignment, hierarchy, whitespace, section structures, title treatments, content modules and repeatable editorial layouts for short- and long-form communication.

05 / Digital

Components

Reusable website patterns (navigation, buttons, links, cards, content sections, calls to action, forms) and responsive behaviour across desktop, tablet and mobile.

06 / Applications

Templates

Reusable presentation and pitch-deck layouts, social and announcement formats, document covers, insight and report layouts, and selected Forum communication applications within scope.

07 / Motion

Interaction principles

Where needed, a restrained approach to transitions, animation and interactive states, so digital experiences feel consistent with the brand. Motion supports clarity rather than decoration.

08 / Accessibility

Consistency

Practical rules for readable type sizes, sufficient colour contrast, clear interaction states and consistent component behaviour. Final implementation requirements confirmed during design and development.

[ How it connects to the ten deliverables ] The design system is not an unrelated eleventh deliverable. It is the working structure created through colour and typography, visual identity, logo design and brand applications, then documented within the brand guidelines. It ensures the identity can be reused consistently rather than recreated for every new asset.

◆ [ 06 ] Brand in use

One coherent identity. Multiple touchpoints.

The identity should be designed as a flexible system so that the Institute feels consistent wherever its audiences encounter it. A supporting design system will define the reusable rules and components behind those applications.

Priority applications
01Institutional presence

Website and core digital touchpoints introducing the Institute and its purpose.

02Presentations

Reusable pitch-deck layouts for introductions, partnerships and institutional communication.

03Public visibility

Social and announcement graphics that build recognition and consistency.

04Forum presence

Event-facing applications that connect clearly to the parent institution.

05Core documents

A consistent visual language for official and institutional materials.
